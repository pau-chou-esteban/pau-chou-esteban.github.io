Independent Research — Concert Ticket Markets

▾

Experimental economics paper replicating a posted-offer market. Co-authored with Louis Gui. VeconLab, 36 participants.

Working PaperVeconLab36 Participants

Research Overview

  • Co-designed and ran an economics experiment with Louis Gui, stemming from our Fed Challenge research
  • Replicated a posted-offer market using VeconLab to simulate concert ticket resale dynamics
  • Found that traded prices fell below equilibrium — a result that contradicts prior literature
  • Traced the discrepancy to violations of three precepts of Smith's Induced Value Theory
  • Co-authored a full manuscript documenting our experimental design, results, and analysis

Abstract

This paper examines price formation in a posted-offer market designed to simulate concert ticket resale platforms, where sellers set prices and buyers decide whether to accept them without negotiation. The experiment was conducted using VeconLab with 36 student participants across eight trading rounds. Transaction prices increased over the course of the experiment, but most trades occurred below the theoretical equilibrium price and overall market efficiency was approximately 75%. This result differs from prior posted-offer market experiments. The divergence is likely explained by violations of the monotonicity, salience, and dominance precepts of induced value theory.

Chou-Esteban, A., & Gui, L. (2026). Pricing Without Negotiation: Experimental Evidence from a Simulated Concert Ticket Market [Unpublished manuscript]. The Bronx High School of Science.
